% Suggested text for the results section:
On the 120 WebQA test questions, MemGraphRAG-V reaches a mean Document-ID Recall@5 of 67.50\%
(95\% grouped-bootstrap interval [44.44\%, 81.35\%]; 17 shared-gold-document groups) and a
supplied-image Recall@5 of 63.75\% (Table~\ref{tab:webqa-test}). Compared on the same questions,
it improves recall by $+26.25$ points over text-only dense retrieval and by
$+8.33$ points over caption-augmented dense retrieval (Table~\ref{tab:webqa-paired}). Both paired
intervals include zero, so with only 17 independent document groups these improvements are
indicative rather than statistically established.

The reader answered ``Insufficient evidence'' for 60 of the 120 questions, including 28 for which
all gold documents had been retrieved, and otherwise produced short answers (median one word).
WebQA references are full sentences, so checking whether the reference appears in the prediction
matches only one answer (0.83\%). Checking the reverse direction, whether the short prediction
appears in the reference, matches 34 answers (28.33\%). Both are lexical heuristics: the second
misses near-synonyms (``fish'' for ``catfish'') and neither is a judged accuracy. Reader
abstention, not retrieval alone, is therefore the main limit on answer quality.
